# Representation Models

This directory collects **working representation models** that emerge from laboratory work, field observations, or repeated practical experience.

A model is not automatically a finding, law, or validated method. It is a reusable structure for describing a class of representation problems in a way that can later be tested, refined, rejected, or promoted.

## Model discipline

Each model should state:

- the class of underlying situations it is intended to describe;
- the question or decision it is meant to support;
- the information the representation must preserve;
- the information it may omit or defer;
- the recovery path for omitted information;
- the relevant costs of representation, transmission, inspection, and recovery;
- the failure condition;
- the current claim status and evidence level.

Models should remain distinguishable from:

- **Representation Records**, which preserve question-specific evidence;
- **field observations**, which record concrete episodes that may generate hypotheses;
- **experiments**, which test frozen questions and criteria;
- **principles**, which may later summarize evidence across multiple models or records.
