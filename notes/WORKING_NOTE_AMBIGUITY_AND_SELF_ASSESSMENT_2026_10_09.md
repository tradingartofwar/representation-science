# Working note — ambiguity load, perceived capability, and self-doubt

**Date:** 2026-10-09
**Status:** HYPOTHESIS / conceptual distinction, not a verified cognitive law

## Question

Could self-doubt sometimes be a compact interpretation of a temporary reduction in cognitive fluency, rather than an accurate assessment of enduring capability?

## Candidate model

A person handling many unresolved, ambiguous contexts may experience difficulty quickly retrieving intentions, reconstructing the next step, or shifting between problems. That experienced friction might be compressed into a more general feeling such as “I am less capable.” The compression can omit the distinction between a temporary operating constraint and overall ability.

Candidate chain (not established): competing unresolved contexts → increased retrieval/context-switching burden → reduced subjective fluency → negative self-assessment → additional felt pressure.

The direction of influence may be reciprocal or confounded; no mechanism is established.

## Consequential distinctions

- Total activity volume differs from the number of unresolved contexts.
- Retrieval difficulty differs from loss of stored information.
- Temporary fluency differs from durable capability.
- Feeling less capable differs from independently measured performance.
- Self-doubt may be an interpretation of experience rather than an accurate capacity measure.
- Releasing deliberate carry is different from completing or abandoning external obligations.
- Reducing simultaneous ambiguity differs from stopping work.
- A useful short-term strategy does not prove its proposed mechanism.

## Candidate low-pressure approach

Choose one bounded task; let other responsibilities remain in their owning records; avoid imposing artificial urgency; identify only the next visible step. Evaluate whether subjective coherence and access to work improve, without declaring improvement in advance.

## Relation to existing research

Connects to representational cost, carry burden, state capture, and the distinction between what a representation expresses and what reality supports.

The important question is whether self-doubt represents durable capability accurately, or whether it sometimes compresses a local, context-dependent performance constraint into a misleading global judgment.

## Evidence and limits

This note is an abstract working hypothesis motivated by naturalistic discussion. No personal health details, names, private events, or measurements are included. Existing terminology such as ambiguity tax is conceptual rather than an independently measured quantity.

Possible tests should distinguish ambiguity load, task-switch frequency, recall latency, self-reported fluency and objective output under ordinary low-risk conditions. Preserve null and disconfirming cases. No experiment or monitoring is activated.

> **A compressed self-assessment can omit the distinction between temporary difficulty accessing capability and a change in capability itself.**
