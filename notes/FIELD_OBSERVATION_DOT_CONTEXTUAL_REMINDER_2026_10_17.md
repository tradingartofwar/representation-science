# Field observation — Dot surfaced a forgotten calendar commitment

**Date recorded:** October 17, 2026  
**Status:** User-confirmed practical value; mechanism and counterfactual not established.  
**Source:** Screenshot of a dot1 message supplied in Mission Control and the user's immediate explanation. Keep the screenshot in the originating conversation; do not assume a repository copy.

## Observed notification

Dot reported a calendar change worth flagging: an orthodontist appointment for Arius on Monday, October 19, 9–10 AM Pacific. It also mentioned a WABA call from 1–2 PM and a previously discussed 8 AM Maple Valley pickup. Dot noted that the calendar entry did not list the appointment location or driver.

The screenshot supports that Dot **reported** these details; this record does not independently verify the calendar events, whether the WABA call recurs monthly, or the transportation arrangements. It also does not establish a direct time conflict.

## User confirmation of consequence

The user said this was something they **had not noticed**: they had forgotten about the monthly meeting. They believed it might be listed as a monthly meeting but were uncertain. Dot's proactive message recovered a commitment from outside the user's active attention.

**Grounded outcome:** useful awareness of a forgotten commitment. Not established: a prevented missed meeting, a solved scheduling conflict, or any particular future action.

## Representation Science relevance

Working hypothesis: useful proactive assistance may depend on retaining relationships across calendar entries, conversational context and unresolved logistics, rather than simply retrieving isolated facts. This case illustrates a candidate process: retrieve/report events, relate them to other commitments, identify an uncertainty, and surface a potentially consequential relationship.

Distinguish:
1. Reported facts (the screenshot's message).
2. User-confirmed value (the forgotten meeting was brought to attention).
3. Inference (Dot may have linked calendar and conversation context).
4. Unknown mechanism (exact data sources, retrieval steps, ranking/selection rules, whether recurrence metadata was read).
5. Unknown counterfactual (what would have happened without the notification).

## Follow-up correction: transportation plan and stale context

On October 17 the user clarified the earlier intended plan: arrive in Maple Valley at 8 AM to drive the family member to a 9 AM Tacoma appointment, with an allowance for a possible 15-minute delay. The reported 1–2 PM meeting was expected to occur after returning home. The user's subsequent update was that another family member had picked up the patient the previous day and would handle the morning drop-off; the user now expected to collect the patient after the appointment and bring them home. The precise return pickup time was not specified.

**Interpretation:** Dot's alert connected the old 8 AM pickup plan to the appointment and the later meeting, but did not reflect the updated transport assignment. The observed issue is potentially **state freshness / update propagation**, not necessarily missing relational reasoning. Distinguish the previously intended plan, the current reported plan, and whether Dot had access to the update at notification time; the latter is unknown. No calendar edit or Dot notification has been performed by this research note.
