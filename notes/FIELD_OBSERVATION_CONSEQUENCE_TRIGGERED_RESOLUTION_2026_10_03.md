# Field observation — consequence-triggered resolution and return to reality

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Status:** OBSERVATION / HYPOTHESIS — not established as a general cognitive mechanism

## Observation

A familiar house had been represented in memory as broadly yellow-toned. That representation had been adequate for ordinary interaction because no consequential task required finer color discrimination.

A later task changed the demand: select paint samples that actually match the exterior. Yellowish samples were chosen from memory. Direct comparison with the house showed the exterior was not well represented by that recollection and was comparatively more pink-toned than expected.

The important point is not merely that memory was inaccurate. The coarse representation had not previously needed to preserve that distinction.

## Candidate Representation Science principle

> **Resolution should increase when consequence demands it. When the active representation cannot support the required resolution, return to the source rather than assuming deeper retrieval will recover the answer.**

This suggests a distinction between:

- **adequacy for ordinary orientation** — a coarse reconstruction may be sufficient;
- **adequacy for a consequential decision** — finer distinctions may suddenly matter;
- **confidence in reconstruction** — subjective certainty does not establish that the representation has the resolution required by the current task;
- **contact with reality** — when the demanded resolution exceeds what the active representation supports, direct source comparison can correct the model.

## Relation to reconstruction

This observation is compatible with, but does not prove, a reconstructive-memory view.

A useful working possibility is:

experience → retained relational/generative traces → present demand → reconstructed representation → action

rather than:

experience → complete stored representation → literal retrieval.

If so, the failure mode is not necessarily poor storage. It may be a mismatch between the **resolution of the reconstruction** and the **resolution demanded by the present consequence**.

## Relation to EXP-001

EXP-001 distinguished missing explicit support from information that remained internally reconstructible. This field observation raises a neighboring question:

> When should a system attempt internal reconstruction, and when should it stop reconstructing and reconnect with reality?

This extends the current experimental object:

payload + decoder + requested output + trust/recovery contract

with a possible additional variable:

**required resolution under present consequence.**

## Research questions

1. How can a system estimate the resolution actually supported by its current representation?
2. How can it detect that a present decision requires finer distinctions?
3. What signals should trigger internal reconstruction versus external source recovery?
4. Can confidence be calibrated to representational resolution rather than familiarity?
5. Can this be tested in a bounded task with independently checkable ground truth?
6. When does preserving a recovery path outperform preserving finer detail continuously?

## Boundary

This is a field observation from one everyday episode. It does not establish a neurological theory of memory, a general cognitive law, or a universal decision rule. Its value is as a concrete hypothesis generator for Representation Science.
