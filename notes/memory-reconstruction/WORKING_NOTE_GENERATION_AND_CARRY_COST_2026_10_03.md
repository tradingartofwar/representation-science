# Working note — generation, carry cost, and model-based reconstruction

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Status:** HYPOTHESIS MAP / exploratory synthesis  
**Boundary:** does not establish a neurological mechanism or universal cognitive law.

## New distinction: generation cost is not carry cost

Recent discussion suggests that the word **cost** has been conflating several very different things.

Vance reports that richly imagined scenes can be generated with very low experienced effort: the Grand Canyon, whales moving through the air, a nuclear explosion in the distance, the backyard, birds, walking down the road, or large-scale astronomical scenes can all be composed rapidly.

By contrast, unresolved responsibilities, deadlines, promises, exact state, and "do not forget this" obligations have obvious persistent carry cost.

This suggests separating at least:

- **experienced generation effort** — how hard it feels to produce a representation;
- **latency** — how quickly the representation becomes available;
- **persistent carry cost** — burden of maintaining unresolved state across time;
- **precision/provenance cost** — burden of keeping exact reality-linked distinctions intact;
- **verification cost** — effort required to return to the source;
- **substrate/metabolic cost** — physical implementation cost, which may not track subjective effort.

Do not infer low biological energy consumption from low subjective effort.

## Possible architectural implication

A useful human-AI division may be:

**Human strengths**
- rapid generative recombination;
- intuition;
- meaning;
- valuation;
- flexible scene construction;
- rich person/place models.

**External / AI strengths**
- persistent exact state;
- unresolved dependencies;
- chronology;
- provenance;
- source recovery;
- return conditions;
- precision over time.

Possible design principle:

> Offload what is costly to carry continuously, not necessarily what is easy to regenerate on demand.

## Stable model + current context + exceptions

A recurring intuition is that familiar people, places, and routines may not require full episode-by-episode storage at equal fidelity.

For a familiar person such as Shui, a mature model can support likely regeneration of:
- habits;
- preferences;
- typical reactions;
- voice characteristics;
- common goals;
- routine behavior.

A candidate efficient structure is:

**stable model + current context + consequential deviations / exceptions → generated representation**

This would imply that highly predictable material can be regenerated, while surprising or consequential deviations deserve stronger episode-specific preservation.

The important research question is therefore not simply "what can be regenerated?" but:

> What would be reconstructed incorrectly if we relied on the stable model alone?

That identifies the information that may need explicit persistence.

## Exception importance

Potentially valuable stored material includes:

- prediction errors;
- novel events;
- departures from routine;
- changed preferences;
- unusual reactions;
- commitments;
- exact arbitrary facts;
- deadlines;
- decisions whose provenance matters;
- evidence needed to distinguish memory from generation.

This is only a candidate architecture. The current observations do not establish that biological memory literally stores "models plus exceptions."

## Vivid dreaming as a cautionary comparison

Vivid dreams illustrate that extremely rich conscious representations can occur without corresponding physical action and without feeling like costly deliberate construction.

This supports only the phenomenological distinction:
- representational richness can be high;
- subjective generative effort can be low.

It does **not** establish that neural/metabolic computation is cheap.

## Competing models remain open

Do not collapse the observations into one explanation.

Keep at least these possibilities live:

1. detailed storage with partial access;
2. sparse episodic traces plus reconstruction;
3. relational graph / node navigation;
4. stable generative models plus episode-specific exceptions;
5. retrieval seeds feeding local reconstruction;
6. hybrid systems combining several of the above.

The correct posture is:

> preserve distinctions, generate competing models, and seek observations that discriminate among them.

## Relation to Representation Science

The broader Representation Science question may be shifting from:

> What must be stored?

toward:

> What must remain persistently carried, what can be regenerated safely, what must remain externally recoverable, and what must be re-grounded in reality before action?

This is still exploratory.

The strongest current distinction is:

> **High generative capacity does not imply high persistent carry capacity.**

That distinction may matter for both cognitive theory and Synergistic Intelligence design.
