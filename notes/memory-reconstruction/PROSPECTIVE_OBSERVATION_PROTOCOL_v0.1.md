# MEM-OBS-002 — prospective consequence and recall observation

**Prepared:** 2026-10-06. **Version:** proposed v0.1.  
**State:** PROTOCOL PREPARATION ONLY — no day enrolled, no activity map collected, no observation begun, no event-specific questions selected or disclosed.  
**Claim status:** HYPOTHESIS for a salience/consequence association; OPEN for the observation and every explanatory mechanism.  
**Study type:** prospective, single-participant field observation; not a controlled experiment, clinical assessment or neurological model.

This is the requested next design after the EXP-002 execution/stop disposition was documented. It is independent of that trial's unresolved replay integrity and does not treat the trial as certified successful. Before starting, discuss this protocol and its contamination limits with Vance. A later explicit start and a prospective registration are required; preparation itself does not enroll today's already experienced events.

## 1. Question and operational meaning

Initial hypothesis: **Do naturally consequential or salient experiences preserve more recoverable distinctions than comparable routine experiences?**

The observable question is narrower: **under recorded delays and a fixed progression of neutral cues, do prospectively registered, naturally consequential/nonroutine episodes yield more independently supported distinctions than comparable routine episodes?**

“Recoverable” means reported under this interview contract. It does not establish what was neurally stored, whether an unreported detail is absent rather than inaccessible, or whether a later detail was recovered rather than constructed. Good source/status annotation and external checks help separate these possibilities without resolving their mechanism.

Practical consequence, subjective importance, novelty, decision making, problem solving and interpersonal meaning remain separate descriptors. Do not combine them into one unexamined salience score or assume that more consequence must improve every memory measure. Vividness, quantity, accuracy, relations, confidence and cue dependence may diverge.

## 2. Prospective boundary and pilot cap

Use **one future ordinary day**, with at most **four pre-registered activity windows** and at most **two within-day comparisons**. This is an inspectability bound, not a power calculation. No automatic extension to another day to obtain a preferred contrast.

At activation, Vance may supply only a coarse activity map: broad activities, rough windows and which are ordinarily familiar. Do not ask for intended purchases, routes, meeting topics, people, object attributes, decisions or other details that could later become tested content. The coarse map is a recruitment/registration aid, not a rehearsal or an answer key. Preserve any incidental detail volunteered in a contamination log; do not later call that detail unprimed.

Before the first eligible window begins, freeze a case registration containing:

- date, local timezone, up to four episode IDs, broad activity categories and non-overlapping start/end rules;
- planned duration ranges and the recall appointment, selected without referring to future memory performance;
- the deterministic matching rule below, interview order, evidence routes and access separation;
- whether naturally generated evidence can be retained without additional attention-directing behavior;
- deviations/abort rules and where private records will be kept.

All four windows must be registered before target experience. Use the earliest candidate windows in the coarse map that plausibly support same-category comparisons and ordinary external evidence. Do not recruit only an event Vance later calls memorable. An unanticipated event inside a registered window remains part of that window; one outside it is an unregistered exploratory anecdote, not a replacement case.

Do not manufacture importance, impose decisions, stage surprises, ask for memorization, or encourage unnecessary photography. Continue ordinary activities safely; no prompts while driving or doing safety-sensitive work. Routine records may be retained, but deliberate new logging or unusually careful retention is itself recorded as a possible encoding intervention.

## 3. Exposure classification and comparability

Do not define a “salient” episode by how much is remembered. Do not label an episode routine because recall is poor.

The primary contrast uses a **separate evidence assessment**, made without viewing the recall transcript if an actual separation can be arranged. It records whether a registered window has a documented consequential choice, problem/departure from routine, changed commitment or other prospectively defined marker. The marker must be supported independently of the participant's later detailed recall. Register the marker categories at activation, not specific predicted incidents. Absence of a record alone does not establish absence of consequence.

A routine comparison needs both the prospectively registered familiar-activity baseline and enough evidence to assess the relevant window. If consequence/nonroutine status cannot be determined, label it UNCERTAIN. Later participant ratings of importance, surprise, emotional/interpersonal meaning and felt consequence are useful **separate self-reports**, collected only after recall is frozen. They are not an independent exposure label and are not used to recut the groups after viewing accuracy.

Deterministic matching rule: order independently classified consequential/nonroutine windows by their registered episode ID; pair each with the lowest-ID unused routine window in the same coarse activity category, with duration within a factor of two and actual end-to-recall delay within two hours. These are practical pilot limits, not validated equivalence thresholds. Preserve exact durations/delays and obvious differences. Reject a pair from the primary contrast if routine status, temporal match or comparable evidence coverage remains uncertain. Keep its observations separately; do not delete it.

If no eligible contrast arises naturally, the result is **contrast unavailable**. If only one pair qualifies, report one pair. Do not add cases or reinterpret an episode to rescue the design. Consequence is not randomized; even a matched association cannot establish that consequence caused better retention.

## 4. Evidence plan and access separation

Possible evidence comes from **ordinary** receipts, transaction records, existing photographs, messages, calendar/timestamp records, retained objects, or a consenting person's independent account. Availability is not equivalent to fidelity:

| Source type | What it can support | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receipt/transaction record | Its own recorded transaction fields and bounded itemization. | Does not exhaust the visit, prove attention, or by itself identify every participant/action. |
| Photograph or other existing capture | Visible content at an evidenced capture moment. | Framing, capture time and selection matter; outside-frame absence is not disproved existence. |
| Message/calendar/timestamp | Recorded statement, plan or system event. | A plan is not proof of occurrence; a message may be retrospective or already rehearsed. |
| Retained object | Its present inspected properties and documented provenance. | Present state may differ from event-time state. |
| Independent recollection | An independently elicited corroborating account. | Fallible corroboration, not documentary ground truth; discuss neither account before each is sealed. |

The recall interviewer must not inspect event evidence before the complete recall freeze. A separate custodian may hold records without showing them to Vance or the interviewer. If there is no separate custodian, leave evidence unopened until all recall is frozen. **One assistant cannot become blinded by promising to ignore facts already read.** If blind evidence coding later matters, use a genuinely separate reviewer/context supplied evidence and registration but no recall. That is a future role specification, not delegation or contact initiated now.

An evidence-only inventory lists what each source can actually establish, its limits, provenance and uncertainty. Make and seal that inventory before the evidence coder sees recall. Include the pre-specified matching/classification judgment there. If clean separation is unavailable, label the coding unblinded and the contrast exploratory; do not claim the bias control was achieved.

No event-specific verification questions or candidate answers are supplied before experience or before the recall freeze. The evidence inventory is created from the evidence under a fixed neutral coding grammar, not by selecting only facts that Vance happened to recall. No participant-facing factual quiz is needed before freezing: compare claims to the inventory afterward.

Do not ask Vance to search his phone, read receipts, inspect objects or consult another person during recall. If ordinary life required such access earlier, retain that fact as a rehearsal/source-exposure covariate rather than pretending clean exposure. Necessary real-world action takes priority; exclude contaminated content from an unassisted-memory claim, not from the audit trail.

This repository is public. Store the generic protocol, blank templates and an appropriately de-identified report here. Agree on private custody before collecting raw personal evidence. Do not upload private conversations, business/client material, identifiers or third-party details into this repository. Do not contact witnesses or send messages without authorization. Publication of future raw data is not authorized by this preparation request.

## 5. Recall sequence — before all verification

At activation, fix one recall appointment after the registered windows, preferably later that day. Record actual event endpoints, elapsed delays, interruptions, unusual distraction and known rehearsal without eliciting target details. Do not change the delay because an event seems memorable. A missed appointment is a deviation, not permission to redefine the interval silently.

**Stage A — broad spontaneous recall, up to ten minutes.** Begin with one non-leading invitation about the registered period: “Tell me what comes back from that period, in whatever order it comes.” Record verbatim order and prompt/time boundaries. Do not supply missing people, objects, transitions, expected surprises or likely decisions. Pauses and “nothing else” are legitimate. Do not reward rich stories or ask for a coherent narrative.

**Stage B — neutral window cues, up to three minutes per registered window.** After Stage A is closed for all windows, use only its registered broad activity/time label. Ask what, if anything, comes to mind for that window. Use a fixed interview order based on the registration (chronological for the first pair, reverse for the second if present). Record the cue verbatim. Newly reported content is cue-assisted, never relabelled spontaneous.

**Stage C — limited expansion, at most two content-neutral invitations per window.** “Anything else?” and, only using the participant's own anchor, “Does anything before or after that come back?” are permitted. Do not name an expected entity, attribute or correct alternative. Questions that happen to direct attention are logged as cues. Stop when the participant declines or reaches the cap; do not keep probing until a desired distinction appears.

After each stage, preserve its transcript before annotation. Then ask the participant to tag the origin/status of the claims already made and give confidence without checking sources. Extra detail arising during annotation gets its own later stage/order marker. Never infer a participant's confidence from tone or vividness.

For every distinct claim, retain:

- claim ID, exact text, episode link if known, retrieval order, stage and preceding prompt;
- participant's source label: **remembered, inferred, schema/routine-based, imagined, uncertain or mixed**;
- confidence in the event-specific claim (0–100 if comfortable, otherwise unspecified), independently of vividness;
- whether the content was an initial retrieval seed, a transition, a temporal/spatial relation, a decision boundary or routine structure, only where actually present;
- any later correction/addition with its own timestamp; never overwrite the original.

“Corroborated” is a **verification status added later**, not a substitute for the original remembered/inferred label. A correct inference remains an inference. A vivid image remains phenomenological content until evidence supports its event-specific claim.

## 6. Complete recall freeze and verification

Before opening evidence, end all pre-verification questioning and save the complete transcript, exact prompts/order, claim ledger, stage boundaries, confidence/status annotations, deviations and access log. Record a timestamp and SHA-256 manifest. Read the saved files back and verify both hashes and expected record counts; do not rely only on a successful save message. This requirement concerns the record, not a cognitive mechanism.

Only after that freeze may evidence be opened and participant salience/importance ratings be collected. Collect the ratings before showing corrective evidence where possible; retain their post-recall status. Do not let corrections backfill the pre-verification ledger. A necessary clarification after evidence exposure is explicitly post-verification and cannot increase the original recall score.

The evidence-only inventory and recall ledger are joined only after their separate freezes. For each claim record supported / contradicted / partially supported / unresolvable, source IDs and exact rationale. If a source fails to mention something, use unresolvable unless its declared scope is exhaustive for that exact absence claim. Conflicting sources remain a conflict; do not select the source that agrees with recall. Other people's independent accounts are sealed before comparison and remain distinguishable from documentary evidence.

If evidence is accidentally displayed or a leading factual detail is introduced before recall is frozen, record the exact time, content and affected window. Preserve the earlier stage but exclude later affected material from the intended unassisted/cue-neutral contrast. Do not restart the same event and call the second attempt clean. Freeze failure, lost records or unresolved provenance blocks a verified result; preserve and stop the affected observation.

## 7. Outcomes and denominators

The coding unit is one independently checkable, episode-specific distinction: an atomic fact or a relation. Repetition/paraphrase counts once. A relation is counted once as a relation, not expanded into arbitrary permutations of its objects. Unclear episode binding remains unclear. Segmentation decisions and disagreements remain inspectable; a fluent paragraph is not automatically many memory units.

The evidence coder inventories all determinable units within the registered source scope, using the same grammar for all windows. Do not choose a fixed number of “best” facts from a rich record, or stretch a routine record to equalize counts. Annotate source coverage and which broad fact/relation facets are genuinely comparable. Incomparable coverage blocks the primary contrast and is itself reported.

Report separately, by episode and recall stage:

1. Number of distinct claims, with source-status counts and supported/contradicted/partial/unresolvable counts.
2. Number of **spontaneous, evidence-supported, participant-labelled remembered distinctions**, separating facts and relations. This is the primary descriptive recovery outcome, not an estimate of neural storage.
3. Supported, contradicted and unresolved proportions with explicit denominators. A claimed accuracy fraction uses only resolved checkable claims; also disclose how many were excluded as unverifiable.
4. Coverage of the evidence inventory, restricted to comparable documented facets. This is **documented-fact coverage**, not completeness of lived experience or the entire memory.
5. Additional supported distinctions after neutral cues, kept separate from spontaneous recovery. Prompt count/time is part of the access contract.
6. Vividness, confidence, retrieval order/seed reports, transition/decision content, duration/delay and participant-rated importance as separate descriptive dimensions. Do not make one quality score.

Evidence-rich consequential events can appear better simply because more can be checked. Routine episodes can appear accurate if only easy claims are verifiable. Neither unverified details nor undocumented omissions should be forced into a truth denominator. Record both missing evidence and missing recall.

A pair with more supported spontaneous remembered distinctions in the consequential window, comparable exposure/evidence and no accuracy tradeoff is **compatible with** the hypothesis for that pair. It is not a causal test or general law. Equal results are a legitimate local null; a routine advantage is a legitimate contrary observation. More detail with more errors, stronger confidence without accuracy, or more cue benefit with weaker spontaneous recall is mixed. Keep every dimension and both pairs; do not resolve disagreement by inventing utility weights or treating facts within one day as independent participants.

With at most two pairs, no population estimate, significance claim or effect-size precision is justified. If consequence labels, comparability or evidence completeness are unresolved, leave the central comparison OPEN. Do not infer an encoding, storage or retrieval mechanism, and do not claim literal “models plus exceptions” storage from this observation.

## 8. Contamination and interpretation risks

| Risk | Planned handling and remaining limit |
| --- | --- |
| Anticipating the study changes attention | No specific targets disclosed and no memorization task. Vance still knows the broad hypothesis; fully incidental encoding cannot be claimed. |
| Selecting an event because it is memorable | Register windows before experience; use independent markers and fixed matching. Do not choose on recall quantity. |
| Calling vivid recall “salient experience” | Keep external consequence markers separate from post-recall subjective salience ratings. Neither is a perfect measure of the other. |
| Extra photographs/logging become rehearsal | Prefer naturally generated evidence; log any study-induced collection and analyze it separately. |
| Interviewer leaks known facts or prior-session models | No event evidence before recall freeze; no importing specific scenes or people from earlier sessions. Log all prompts. |
| Repeated telling or source checking improves later recall | Record retelling/source access; do not count later content as an uncontaminated first retrieval. |
| Evidence coverage differs by consequence | Inventory source scope independently and restrict comparison to comparable facets; otherwise no primary contrast. |
| Duration, delay, familiarity or distractions differ | Match under the stated bounds and report residual differences; observational confounding remains. |
| Confidence tagging and probing create more content | Preserve stage boundaries; later additions never become original spontaneous reports. |
| Public protocol itself cues future attention | Publish generic process only. No episode-specific item bank or expected answers exists at preparation. Broad awareness remains unavoidable. |
