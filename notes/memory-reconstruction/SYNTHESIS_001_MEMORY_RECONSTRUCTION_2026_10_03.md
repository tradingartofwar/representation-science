# Synthesis 001 — memory, reconstruction, salience and adaptive verification

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Status:** exploratory synthesis / hypothesis map  
**Scope:** observations from guided recall, everyday decisions, and contact-with-reality examples  
**Boundary:** this is not a neurological model and does not establish a general law of human memory.

## Why this note exists

The work began as a question about memory: what becomes available when Vance tries to recall something?

The more useful pattern became broader. The observations repeatedly involved:

- how a query is interpreted before recall begins;
- which compact scene or state becomes active first;
- how richer structure is reconstructed from that state;
- how routine schemas and person-models mix with episode-specific content;
- how interest, novelty, consequence and active goals affect what remains recoverable;
- how imagined or inferred content can be distinguished from stronger episodic content;
- and how contact with reality changes trust in a representation.

The current value is a set of categories and testable distinctions, not a single explanatory theory.

## Current category map

### 1. Retrieval framing / query construction

A cue is not always used literally. The mind may first decide what the cue means.

Example: "ordinary drive" was interpreted as a drive without an external obligation, which then routed recall toward a particular exploratory trip.

Working question:

> What transformations occur between the presented query and the actual internal retrieval target?

### 2. Retrieval seeds / entry representations

Several places and episodes repeatedly became available through privileged starting states:

- home → front entry area;
- kitchen → first view on entering;
- grocery trip → store entrance;
- leaving the store → unloading/cart transition;
- Tally → the same initial moving visual fragment of the robot.

A retrieval seed may be static or dynamic. It appears to act as a compact launch point into a larger structure.

See: [retrieval-seed working note](WORKING_NOTE_RETRIEVAL_SEEDS_2026_10_03.md).

### 3. Episode-level default access vs local-scene access

The whole grocery trip defaulted to the store entrance even when prompted from a later cookie scene. Yet specifically named local scenes could be accessed directly without replaying the intervening trip.

Possible distinction:

- episode identity can have a default access point;
- salient local scenes can remain independently addressable.

### 4. Scene reconstruction and temporal unfolding

Once a sufficiently concrete episodic anchor was reached, memory sometimes unfolded as "being in the scene in a sequence" rather than as a bundle of facts.

The back-seat grocery arrangement was the clearest example.

### 5. Relational spread

A concrete action cue could activate nearby structure.

Example: stabilizing the Coke carton led to bourbon, cookies, console placement, closing the door, getting into the driver's seat, repositioning cookies, and the hunger/cookie exchange.

Working possibility:

> reconstruction may proceed through relational neighborhoods rather than isolated record lookup.

### 6. Schema and procedural memory

Routine environments often produced procedures rather than unique episodes.

At the Costco return store, the remembered structure included:

- check machines out front;
- notice who is behind the counter;
- begin on the left row;
- scan row by row;
- look for novelty, household usefulness or value;
- inspect promising items;
- leave.

This is a reusable search policy, not one particular visit.

### 7. Inquiry strength / attention budget

Some curiosities trigger only a low-cost probe.

The clothing section example showed:

standing curiosity → token look → no deeper investigation → move on.

This suggests attention may be allocated proportionally to the strength of the active inquiry or expected consequence.

### 8. Episodic scaffold vs reconstructed attributes

A remembered action structure can be firmer than its object details.

The shirt episode had a fairly concrete action scaffold—standing, reaching, holding, evaluating—while exact shirt appearance and some attributes were less certain and partly generated.

### 9. Decision-boundary memory

In the shirt episode, the strongest remembered structure was:

possible option → inspect → not a real option → move on.

The decision outcome felt more certain than many sensory details.

Possible hypothesis:

> representations may preferentially preserve distinctions that mattered for action rather than uniform perceptual fidelity.

### 10. Importance / salience gating

Vance proposed a stronger organizing hypothesis:

> detailed episodic memory seems to be available mainly when the system implicitly marked the moment as important.

Candidate sources of importance observed in this work include:

- novelty;
- surprise;
- practical consequence;
- active curiosity;
- interpersonal meaning;
- problem solving;
- change in goal;
- decision boundaries.

This remains a hypothesis and needs controlled contrasts.

### 11. Person-model contribution

Some episode reconstructions appeared to combine event-specific content with stable knowledge about Shui:

- habits;
- preferences;
- likely reactions;
- voice characteristics;
- current perceived state.

This suggests a useful distinction between:

- what is specific to the episode;
- what is regenerated from a stable model of the person.

### 12. Observation vs interpretation

Repeatedly, sparse remembered observations supported richer current interpretations.

Examples:

- a shopper stopping and remaining relatively expressionless;
- interpreting that as surprise, novelty or being in a hurry;
- Shui looking around;
- interpreting her as considering what she might want.

The participant often retained awareness of which layer was observed and which was inferred.

### 13. Representative reconstruction

Some images looked specific but were recognized as likely generated representatives rather than literal episodic replay.

Example: the specific black shirt image.

Important distinction:

> concreteness of imagery is not sufficient evidence that the image is an accurate replay.

### 14. Source/status tagging of representations

Across multiple examples, Vance distinguished representations by status:

- remembered;
- inferred;
- imagined;
- habitual;
- desired;
- observed;
- uncertain;
- corroborated;
- reality-checked.

This may be more important for good decisions than preventing generated content from appearing at all.

### 15. Reality-weighting / correction loop

A recurring operating pattern is:

intuition or possibility → inspect → distinguish observation from inference/imagination → test against reality when needed → keep, revise or discard.

This is better treated as a higher-level control process than as a memory-specific category.

### 16. Consequence-sensitive resolution

The house-paint example showed that a coarse representation may remain adequate until a task requires finer resolution.

Initial state:
- rough remembered color;
- low-cost sample-guessing;
- no major consequence yet.

After contact with reality:
- selected samples were not close;
- evidence showed the memory representation was inadequate for color matching.

As the actual paint deadline approached:
- consequence of another miss increased;
- direct source sampling became preferable;
- policy changed to "no more guessing."

The useful distinction is therefore not merely confidence.

Verification pressure can rise because of:

- evidence that the representation has failed;
- rising cost of another error;
- reduced time or opportunity for retries;
- low cost of checking the source.

### 17. Adaptive representation / recovery policy

A stronger candidate principle from the paint episode:

> use the cheapest representation suitable for current consequence; let contact with reality expose inadequacy; then change the representation or source-access policy.

The system need not know all adequacy limits in advance if it can detect and learn from mismatch.

### 18. Model maturity / experience density

The soup example provides a contrasting case.

Vance predicted with about 95% confidence that a newly made soup would be good because it deliberately emulated a well-practiced prior recipe. The prediction held.

The mismatch was narrow:
- fresh soup was less blended than the remembered rested/overnight state.

This suggests repeated practice can support a mature generative model that performs well without expensive verification.

Possible variable:

> experience density / model maturity may reduce the need for source checking in familiar domains.

## Strongest methodological lessons so far

1. Do not infer storage mechanism from introspection.
2. Do not embed expected scene details in prompts.
3. Separate spontaneous content from prompt-induced expansion.
4. Prefer concrete real episodes over increasingly subtle introspective reports.
5. Separate episode-specific content from schemas, habits and person-models.
6. Record uncertainty and status labels explicitly.
7. Freeze predictions or recall before reality checks.
8. Use external evidence where possible.
9. Treat second-person corroboration as corroboration, not documentary ground truth.
10. Preserve gaps instead of forcing a continuous story.
11. When testing importance gating, compare salient and routine episodes prospectively rather than selecting only memorable events after the fact.

## Current working architecture

A provisional behavioral sequence is:

**demand / cue  
→ query interpretation  
→ retrieval seed or schema  
→ local reconstruction / relational spread  
→ inference and model completion  
→ source/status assessment  
→ action or verification decision  
→ contact with reality  
→ representation / trust / recovery-policy update**

This is a map of observed functions, not a claim about neural implementation.

## Highest-value future questions

When work resumes, prioritize tests that discriminate among competing explanations.

1. **Seed stability:** does the same event return to the same seed days or weeks later under neutral cues?
2. **Importance gating:** do salient and routine events differ prospectively in episodic detail when exposure time is similar?
3. **Schema vs episode:** when does a place cue return procedure first versus a unique event?
4. **Verification threshold:** how do consequence, prior failure, deadline pressure, verification cost and model maturity jointly affect source checking?
5. **Reality calibration:** how often do strong visual reconstructions correspond to externally verified details?
6. **Person-model regeneration:** which features of a remembered interaction are episode-specific versus reconstructed from the stable person model?
7. **Goal effects:** does an active value or goal alter what becomes salient and later retrievable from the same episode?
8. **Generated-content tagging:** how reliably can imagined, inferred and remembered content be distinguished before external verification?

## Pause state

This line of work is intentionally paused after the October 3 exploratory session.

**Return condition:** resume when there is either:

- a naturally occurring recent episode with independent external evidence suitable for a pre-recall / post-verification comparison; or
- a deliberate desire to test one of the discriminating questions above.

Do not turn these observations into a general theory of memory without stronger controlled and externally checked evidence.


## Addendum — generation versus carry

Later discussion added a major cost distinction. Rich imagined representations can be generated with very low subjective effort, while unresolved responsibilities, exact state, deadlines and return obligations have substantial persistent carry cost.

This suggests separating generation effort, latency, persistent carry cost, precision/provenance cost, verification cost and substrate/metabolic cost rather than treating them as one quantity.

A related candidate architecture is **stable model + current context + consequential exceptions → regenerated representation**. For familiar people or routines, predictable structure may be regenerated from a mature model, while prediction errors, unusual deviations, exact arbitrary facts and commitments may deserve stronger explicit persistence.

These possibilities remain open alongside detailed-storage, sparse-trace, relational-network and hybrid models. See [generation/carry working note](WORKING_NOTE_GENERATION_AND_CARRY_COST_2026_10_03.md).
