### 1. A stopping reason can change the meaning of the output

We did not observe a retrieval ceiling. We observed an account stopped by its author, accompanied by a claim that more could be reported. Coding all unsupplied episodes or attributes as forgotten would be unjustified.

A useful extension to future recording is to retain the reason a report ends: voluntary stopping, time/cost, loss of interest, task misunderstanding, no further content available under that prompt, or interruption. Do not infer the reason from transcript length.

Even an unrestricted invitation does not make a report exhaustive. Nor does a longer report establish more accurate retained information. This is a measurement correction within this project, not a claim of scientific novelty.

### 2. Reported relations are candidates for question-specific adequacy

The account links an intended purpose to selection of information for another person's role. Elsewhere, it links a favorable interpretation to a self-instruction intended to protect later conduct. These are abstract descriptions of the reported structure, not endorsements of the event facts.

Such a representation might support different questions: what happened, why an action was chosen, what changed, or what should guide a next decision. A compressed event list could omit the reasons needed for one of those questions while still serving another.

**HYPOTHESIS:** preserving decision-relevant relations may matter more than preserving additional descriptive attributes for some future tasks. The source/status of those relations matters: a plausible later explanation is not automatically a remembered event-time reason. No downstream adequacy comparison was performed here.

### 3. Consequence and local discrepancy need not share a scale

The presence of reported corrections, unmet expectations, and incidental detail inside a routine activity motivates separating overall stakes from local departure from expectation. A low-stakes episode may contain a personally relevant distinction.

This does not establish a preference for storing exceptions. Alternative explanations include rehearsal, ordinary narrative selection, familiarity, conversation, reconstruction, exposure duration, and the study theme itself. The transcript cannot choose among them.

The frozen October 6 windows and classification rules stay unchanged. A finer-grained contrast would require a new prospective design, with units fixed before scoring, rather than retrospectively selecting vivid moments from this account.

### 4. A day boundary is an access question, not demonstrated erasure

P1's statement about leaving the previous day behind is evidence of the participant's experience of changing days. It is not evidence of a biological reset or proof of inaccessible storage.

A future day-boundary study would have to separate delay, sleep, changed goals, cueing, willingness, rehearsal and repeated-test effects. Recalling the same episode twice changes the conditions of its second retrieval. No such study begins with this reflection.

## Relationship to the program

EXP-001 established within its exact mathematical setting that a limited procedure can fail while richer output remains reconstructible from the retained object. Here, the parallel is a **measurement caution only**: a bounded report does not identify the full capability of the source producing it. This does not reproduce the mathematical result in human memory or identify a human decoder.

The existing generation/carry-cost note already separates producing an account from maintaining exact state over time. This session adds a concrete reason to record the cost and stopping policy of communicating an account. No rate, burden or efficiency advantage was measured.

The Question Capacity Frontier note concerns faithfully maintaining the structure of a question. This session illustrates why question purpose, requested detail and acceptable stopping conditions need an explicit contract. It does not establish a capacity frontier or an AI advantage.

**Working framing:** Which distinctions and relations become reportable under a declared question, cue, delay, source-access policy and reporting budget, and which survive independent checking well enough for the next operation?

This complements the salience hypothesis; it does not replace it or claim it has passed.
