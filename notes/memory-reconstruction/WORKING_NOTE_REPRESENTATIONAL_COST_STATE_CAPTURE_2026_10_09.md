# Working note — representational cost, consequence coupling, and state capture

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** WORKING DISTINCTIONS / HYPOTHESIS MAP — no independent measurement or established cognitive mechanism  
**Source type:** synthesis of recent conversation, with one prior first-person imagery report and hypothetical contrasting examples  
**Privacy:** generalized, without private family, medical, business, or client records

## Why this note exists

Earlier research separated the **experienced cost of generating** a representation from the **persistent cost of carrying** exact obligations and relationships. A follow-on discussion raised a third family of costs:

> **What if a representation is easy to generate and not being deliberately maintained, yet the person remains caught in the feeling or state it activated?**

The progression of ideas matters:

1. Rich imaginary scenes can be experienced as available almost immediately and with little conscious generative effort.
2. Precise, consequential, externally constrained problems may impose large ongoing attention and verification burdens.
3. Imagination is not consequence-free *as experienced*: an imagined threat may evoke real fear even if the threatened event has not occurred.
4. The cost of fear need not be **conscious carry**. The person may want to leave the episode behind, but an altered state can persist after the vivid image is no longer actively pictured.
5. Therefore the cost of **holding a representation in mind** and the cost of **being held in a state** are different candidate variables.

This last distinction is the main new contribution. It should not be collapsed into the older generation-versus-carry framework.

## Two contrasting illustrations

### A. Elaborate, apparently low-effort imaginative construction

A previously documented first-person report describes rapidly composing a vast landscape with extraordinary objects and events, and freely modifying the apparent scene. The imagery feels rich and easy to generate.

Under the purely imaginary task, the scene does not have to satisfy exact engineering, geometric, physical or evidentiary constraints. The experience can be coherent enough for imagination without every physical detail being explicit or independently checked.

**What was reported:** high subjective visual richness, fast availability, and low *experienced* effort.

**What was not measured:** neural computation, biological energy, exact geometry encoded, complete physical simulation, or comparable machine-generation output.

### B. A small but consequential operational ambiguity

A short ambiguous question in an institutional workflow may impose substantial burden despite requiring no impressive visualization: several requirements must be jointly satisfied, relevant facts must remain exact, outside sources may need checking, and an error may have practical consequences. Responsibility may remain unresolved for days.

This illustrates potential **joint-constraint, reality-binding, verification, and carry costs**. It is not a measured cross-substrate efficiency comparison.

The comparison is intentionally between **different operations**, not two measurements of identical tasks. It motivates designing fair comparisons rather than establishing which substrate is intrinsically cheaper.

### C. An imagined threat that is difficult to escape

A child imagines a frightening figure in a room. The scene may be simple to construct yet evoke fear, alertness, and a reluctance to sleep. The person may no longer consciously picture the figure but still experience the room as threatening.

Adults may likewise become absorbed in imagined loss, rejection, danger, or catastrophe. This is not inherently a childhood-only phenomenon.

**Important:** these are conceptual examples, not documented observations of a particular child or proof of a universal age pattern.

The key inference is limited:

> **The represented event may be imaginary while the emotional experience and behavioral effects are real.**

## Candidate cost dimensions

The term **cost** is underdetermined unless its unit, bearer, time horizon, question, and consequence are stated. Different dimensions should remain separate:

| Dimension | Question | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generation effort | How difficult does it feel to produce the representation? | Subjective report |
| Availability latency | How long before the representation becomes accessible? | Potentially measurable |
| Specification or fidelity cost | Which details must be generated or explicitly preserved for the requested output? | Task-dependent |
| Joint-constraint cost | Which relationships must be simultaneously consistent? | Task-dependent |
| Reality-binding and verification cost | What must be checked against external sources before reliance or action? | Task-dependent |
| Deliberate carry cost | What effort is spent consciously maintaining exact or unresolved state? | Subjective/behavioral |
| **Consequence coupling** | How strongly does the system treat a representation as important, dangerous, or action-relevant? | Provisional explanatory variable |
| **Involuntary state persistence** | Does the activated experience continue without deliberate maintenance? | Observable/reportable possibility; mechanism unknown |
| **Disengagement cost** | How difficult or slow is it to return to another task or a different felt state? | Candidate measurable outcome |
| Downstream experience and action cost | What attention, behavior, bodily response, or missed opportunity follows? | Depends on outcome measures |
| Substrate/resource cost | What metabolic, computational, or financial resources are physically consumed? | Requires independent measurement |

No single scalar or ordering of these costs is justified. A low value in one dimension does not imply a low value in another.

## Provisional terms

### Consequence coupling

**Working definition:** the extent to which a representation, or the interpretation assigned to it, recruits systems of salience, threat appraisal, expectation, attention and potential action.

This does *not* mean that a known biological module can be identified from the introspective account. Nor does it imply that more vivid images are necessarily more consequential.

Possible determinants include perceived likelihood, severity, personal stakes, familiarity, control, social context, current arousal and implicit processes. These are proposed variables, not measured in the examples.

### State capture

**Working definition:** an episode in which a representation or cue is associated with a persistent state that constrains attention, feeling or behavior even when the person is not deliberately holding the triggering representation in mind.

The key caution is that the **state itself might persist when its originating representation no longer does**. Another explanation is that the representation reactivates intermittently without being reported. The examples do not distinguish those mechanisms.

### Disengagement cost

**Working definition:** the effort, time, interference, or lost opportunity involved in shifting out of the captured state or recovering access to other priorities.

The state may last despite the person *wanting* to disengage. **Trying to stop carrying information** is not the same event as **successfully leaving a state**.

## What changed in the framing

An initial account described imaginary threats as making a representation **costly to experience**. A subsequent user correction emphasized that the experience may be less like *consciously carrying fear* and more like **an episode one cannot escape**.

That revision matters: if the cost is modeled only as deliberate memory maintenance, then persistence of fear after active imagery subsides disappears from the representation. The alternative object of inquiry is a temporally extended state and its effects.

A candidate minimal contrast is:

- **deliberate carry:** maintain item X so as not to forget or misrepresent it;
- **state capture:** remain in condition Y even while trying not to think about X.

The two can coincide, but neither logically entails the other.

## Human–AI collaboration implication

A useful division of representational labor may distribute tasks according to their **measured or reliably observed cost profiles**, rather than the visible complexity of the result.

For example, external systems may relieve deliberate carry by preserving exact statuses, evidence and follow-up conditions; the human may contribute rapid intuitive possibility generation and valuation. But durable storage is not the same as accurate current state, and AI systems can impose new attention costs through unnecessary reporting.

The earlier principle, *offload what is costly to carry continuously*, now needs a complement:

> **Reducing conscious carry may still leave emotional state capture or unwanted attentional burden untouched.**

Any coordination tool that constantly reminds someone of alarming possibilities could reduce memory load while increasing state capture. This is a design hypothesis, not an observed result.

## Bounded research program

1. **Separate outputs.** Specify whether the task is to imagine, describe, render, simulate, recall exactly, decide, or verify. Do not compare low-cost imagination with high-fidelity rendering as though they were identical outputs.
2. **Separate subjective and physical costs.** Where appropriate, collect elapsed time, self-reported effort, fidelity, external checks and downstream attention. Do not infer low neural energy from subjective ease.
3. **Observe state persistence carefully.** For naturally occurring, consented, low-risk cases, note if the original image is still consciously present, whether the feeling remains, when attention becomes available for other tasks, and any uncertainty in timestamps.
4. **Distinguish mechanisms.** Compare ongoing image generation, recurring intrusions, persistent appraisal without imagery, habituation and unrelated background state; no self-report alone can resolve neural implementation.
5. **Keep counterexamples.** Include complex imaginings with little affect, simple thoughts with substantial affect, distress that subsides quickly, and strong feelings not clearly attributable to imagery.
6. **Evaluate opportunity cost.** Ask what other activity or decision became inaccessible during a state, not simply how intense the feeling was.
7. **Test collaboration only on comparable questions.** Human-alone, AI-assisted and AI-alone modes need matched outputs, sources, verification privileges and time/cost measures before comparative claims.
8. **Do not induce distress to produce data.** Use ethical, low-risk protocols if any prospective study is later approved.

No experiment, clinical advice, intervention, monitoring obligation, or change to frozen research contracts is authorized by this note.

## Failure or revision conditions

The working frame requires revision if it cannot distinguish deliberate carry from state persistence in observable tasks; if the terminology merely renames standard constructs without adding a useful question or test; or if a simpler established account predicts the observations equally well.

The naturalistic examples do not establish that unconscious representations are accurate, that threat feelings objectively reflect risk, or that humans outperform particular artificial systems in compute efficiency.

## Working conclusions — not findings

> **A representation may be cheap to generate but expensive to experience.**

> **The cost of carrying information and the cost of being held in a state are different questions.**

> **Representational complexity, computational cost, experienced consequence and disengagement cost should not be collapsed into one variable.**

The next scientific step is to find a bounded case where separating these variables changes what can be observed, predicted or explained.
