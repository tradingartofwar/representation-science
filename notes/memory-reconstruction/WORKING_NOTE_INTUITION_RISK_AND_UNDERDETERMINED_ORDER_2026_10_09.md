# Working note — intuition, implicit risk, and underdetermined sequence

**Recorded:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** REPORTED OBSERVATION / OPEN HYPOTHESES — neither a verified causal account nor a neurological theory  
**Scope:** naturalistic change in felt risk and behavior; relation to delayed question formation; implications for the adequacy of cognitive representations  
**Privacy:** deliberately abstracted. No names, family relationships, health symptoms, exposures, or private conversation details.

## Observation and evidentiary boundary

A participant reported noticing **retrospectively** that, during an ordinary period of activity, their concern about inadvertent contact with a potential source of infection had become less salient. They had not consciously decided to relax precautions or formulated an explicit conclusion that the risk had disappeared. The change was experienced as a **less threatening felt risk**, reflected in less anxious attention to ordinary contact behavior.

Separately, upon waking, a new question about a possible connection between previously known events had entered conscious awareness. The participant investigated that question and learned a relevant but limited fact.

The participant emphasized that **the chronology cannot be resolved**: the reduction in felt risk may have preceded the question, followed it, or emerged through some common process. The participant noticed the behavioral difference only later. No contemporaneous measurement recorded the precise onset of the change, and the experience does not establish whether any implicit judgment was objectively accurate.

Related source: [field observation on delayed question formation](FIELD_OBSERVATION_DELAYED_QUESTION_AND_TARGETED_CHECK_2026_10_09.md). The underlying personally sensitive episode is intentionally not reproduced here.

## The consequential distinctions

1. **Not consciously chosen ≠ known unconscious mechanism.** The absence of an intentional decision does not identify the internal process.
2. **Felt risk ≠ stated belief ≠ actual risk.** These can differ without contradiction.
3. **Retrospectively noticed change ≠ known onset time.** The observation is genuine as a report, but its temporal ordering remains unidentified.
4. **Question causes feeling / feeling causes question / common antecedent.** All remain possible; the reported history cannot distinguish them.
5. **Accurate intuition ≠ explicable intuition.** A judgment can in principle be correct even when the process that produced it is not understood; accuracy still needs evidence independent of the intuition.
6. **Possible accuracy ≠ demonstrated accuracy in this case.** Subsequent facts did not independently establish the cause of the underlying event or the true level of contact risk.
7. **Conscious imagery richness ≠ independently measured representation completeness, computational work, or metabolic cost.** Experienced speed and effort are observations; cross-substrate efficiency comparisons require measurement.

## Competing causal sketches (not adjudicated)

- **Question → felt-risk change → behavior:** new explanatory possibility shifts salience or perceived threat.
- **Felt-risk change → question:** a pre-existing shift in the implicit evaluation helps make a new relational question salient.
- **Common process → both:** reappraisal, priming, habituation, incidental cues, or another unobserved factor influences both.
- **Unrelated timing:** ordinary recovery of calm and an independently arising question happen near each other.

Retrospective report does not select among these graphs.

## Why preserve the possibility of accurate intuition?

Introspection may yield a reliable sense or judgment without access to its generating mechanism. The inability to explain the process is **not evidence of error**; the ability to form a vivid or compelling impression is likewise **not evidence of truth**.

The research obligation is to keep several hypotheses live:

- an intuition can be inaccurate because of missing information, expectation, misleading associations, or coincidence;
- an intuition can be accurate through learned or implicit pattern sensitivity, even when the person cannot articulate the cues;
- the internal mechanisms and representations may be richer or differently organized than the current scientific or computational vocabulary captures.

The third possibility is **not** a finding that humans use an unknown or superior mechanism. It is a refusal to treat today's explanatory models as exhaustive.

An independently verified correct prediction can establish task-specific success without settling how it arose. Reliable above-baseline performance across controlled, prospective cases would support stronger claims. Negative cases, base rates, calibration and selection effects matter.

## Relationship to rapid, low-effort human visualization

Earlier self-reports described the apparent ease and near-immediacy with which elaborate imagined scenes could be assembled and modified: vast landscapes, very large objects, dramatic physical events, and changes in their relations.

This raises an intriguing comparison between **rich, fast subjective generation** and artificial generation of detailed outputs. Yet:

- subjective vividness does not prove that every geometric or physical detail was explicitly encoded;
- imagining a scene and producing an externally inspectable, high-fidelity image or simulation are different requested outputs;
- subjective effort does not quantify neural energy use or computational operations;
- apparent latency and energy costs across biological and machine substrates are not directly comparable without a common task and measurement.

See [generation versus carry cost](WORKING_NOTE_GENERATION_AND_CARRY_COST_2026_10_03.md), which already separates experienced generation effort, latency, persistent carry burden, precision/provenance, verification and metabolic/substrate cost.

## Connection to Representation Science

Two research questions should remain separate:

**Question A — adequacy:** What does an internal representation or implicit working model preserve that makes a judgment available, sometimes without explicit conscious reasoning?

**Question B — causal influence:** How can that model, or a change in the felt meaning of information, affect behavior and downstream outcomes independently of an explicit belief or instruction?

This extends the earlier line:

> source situation → possible implicit representation or process → experienced salience / question / feeling → observable behavior → external check → revised confidence.

The arrows are **candidate relations**, not an established sequence in this episode. Do not force the three middle stages into an order the observation did not preserve.

It is possible for reality to contain distinctions that an investigator's present representation cannot express. This supports methodological openness and correctability, not indiscriminate endorsement of every proposed mechanism.

## How to investigate without overclaiming

For future naturally occurring, consented, privacy-safe episodes:

1. Where practical, timestamp the **first noticing** of a felt change, emerging question or altered behavior, and explicitly mark any unknown onset.
2. Record prospective predictions *before* accessing confirming sources; retain mismatches, null results and failed intuitions.
3. Compare calibrated intuitive judgments with appropriate explicit judgments and chance/base-rate baselines.
4. Separate experienced cost and speed from measured output quality, persistence, and verification cost.
5. Never infer the cause of a person's real illness or the wisdom of an intervention from unmeasured feeling alone.
6. Treat the presence of a novel, seemingly accurate intuition as a **question generator** until independent evidence supports its reliability in the relevant domain.

No intervention, behavioral experiment, health monitoring or public disclosure of personal health data is authorized by this note.

## Summary

> **A judgment may be accurate without its generating process being understood. A behavioral change may indicate that felt risk has shifted without revealing when, why, or whether that shift tracked reality.**

**Claim status: OPEN.** The observation preserves an unresolved temporal and causal distinction and the legitimate possibility of accurate implicit judgments, not proof of either mechanism or accuracy.
