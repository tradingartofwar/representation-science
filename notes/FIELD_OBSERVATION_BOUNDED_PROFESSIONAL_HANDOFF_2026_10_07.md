# Field observation — bounded professional handoff under review cost

**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Status:** FIELD OBSERVATION / hypothesis generator  
**Privacy:** generalized; no private case facts retained

## Observation

A practical matter had accumulated a large history of communications, actions, disputed claims, supporting evidence, and ongoing events. A professional advisor was needed for a limited set of judgments.

The professional's time carried substantial cost. Two naive representations were both unattractive:

1. **send everything** — maximizes completeness but increases review cost and can bury the active question;
2. **send a short narrative conclusion** — minimizes review burden but risks omitting distinctions that could materially change the advice.

The useful reframing was to define both:

- the **information boundary**: what facts must be represented for the current questions; and
- the **participation boundary**: what the professional is and is not being asked to do.

The remaining source material can stay available for targeted recovery if a question exposes the need for finer resolution.

## Representation Science interpretation

The episode suggests that professional handoffs are not merely communication problems. They are **question-specific representation-design problems under asymmetric inspection cost**.

The relevant object may be:

> underlying reality + recipient role + requested question + authorized scope + initial representation + recovery path + inspection cost

This extends the current project emphasis on payload, decoder, requested output, and trust/recovery contract.

## Candidate lesson

> A high-quality handoff preserves the distinctions that can change the recipient's bounded judgment, while deferring recoverable detail whose main effect would be to increase inspection burden.
