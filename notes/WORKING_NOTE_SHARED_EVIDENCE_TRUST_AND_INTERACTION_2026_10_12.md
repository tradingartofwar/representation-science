# Working note — shared evidence, trust, and interaction-sensitive representation

**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Status:** FIELD-MOTIVATED HYPOTHESES / not a causal finding  
**Privacy:** Fully abstracted from a private interpersonal and property-related interaction; no participant identities, addresses, case details, private quotations, or factual claims about a specific dispute are reproduced.

## The question that changed

An initially narrow question asks whether showing someone better evidence changes their conclusion.

A broader question asks: **what must a representation of an interaction preserve to explain or predict whether people can jointly update their understanding and cooperate?**

A sequence reported in a private conversation involved joint observation, discussion of what measurements could and could not establish, voluntary correction of a misunderstanding, acknowledgment of another party's concerns, and later reported improvement in conversation. The narrator explicitly corrected an AI account that had narrowed the possible factors to evidence and listening: **both seemed relevant, and more mattered**.

That correction is the motivation for this note—not proof that any particular ingredient caused the outcome. No external observer, independent outcome measure, or complete interaction record was available for this research note.

## Candidate missing distinctions

1. **Claim versus underlying question.** A global allegation or defense may compress separable questions about observation, measurement, inference, uncertainty, and actionable next steps. Disaggregating them can permit joint examination without first requiring wholesale agreement.
2. **Evidence availability versus jointly witnessed evidence.** The same measurements might affect a discussion differently when participants see collection, limitations, and interpretation together rather than receiving one side's summary.
3. **Epistemic correction versus interpersonal acknowledgment.** Changing an assessment is not equivalent to feeling heard, respected, or understood; both may matter, and neither is established as sufficient.
4. **Component identification versus interaction structure.** A list of factors—evidence, patience, openness, time, candor, acknowledgment, shared interests—does not preserve their order, timing, feedback, or dependencies. What matters may arise from the interaction among components rather than any one factor.
5. **Stated intention to cooperate versus sustained cooperation.** A constructive conclusion to an interaction does not establish durable future behavior.
6. **Technical result versus global conclusion.** Local measurements or observations can narrow particular questions but do not automatically resolve all hidden conditions or broader allegations. Measurement type, scope, controls, and uncertainty remain necessary.
7. **Apparent persuasion versus alternative causes.** Shifts in tone and willingness may reflect social rapport, new data, changed incentives, situational constraints, elapsed time, or other unobserved factors.

## Candidate representation adequacy test

**Underlying system:** An interaction among people examining a disputed real-world condition, with changing evidence, beliefs, commitments, and relationships.

**Question:** Can we distinguish an accurate, temporary local agreement from durable shared understanding or cooperation, and identify which evidence would discriminate plausible explanations?

**Requested output:** A source-limited account of observed change, remaining disagreement, and next measurable consequence—not an attribution of motive or a diagnosis.

**Candidate compact representation:** `new evidence -> belief change -> cooperation`.

**Omitted information:** Whether evidence was mutually observed; interpretive limits; trust and prior interactions; who corrected whom; sequence of exchanges; acknowledged values; incentives; time; uncertainty; and behavior after the interaction.

**Failure trigger:** Two interactions with similar evidence but different relational or behavioral outcomes, or an apparent immediate agreement followed by renewed disagreement. The compact chain cannot explain these differences without enrichment.

**Possible enrichment:** A time-ordered event graph retaining claims, observations, corrections, participant acknowledgments, proposed actions, and independently observed follow-through, with provenance and uncertainty attached to each edge. This is a candidate, not a validated minimal representation.

**Recovery:** The richer account requires actual chronological source material and later outcomes; a retrospective narrative cannot reconstruct all omitted causes.

**Costs:** Additional collection and interpretation require human attention and may change the interaction being observed. No effort, storage, or causal-estimation costs were measured.

## Critical open question

**Can a representation preserve all individually consequential distinctions and still fail because it omits the relationships among them?**

This is related to, but not identical with, simply increasing representational detail. The necessary structure may be relational and temporal: ordering, reciprocal updating, and feedback. A long list of facts can remain inadequate for the question of how an interaction unfolded.

## Counterchecks and possible study

A bounded prospective study could compare separately coded observations, claims, source quality, timing of feedback, expressions of understanding, decisions, and later verified actions. Include cases where shared evidence does not produce agreement, where trust improves without belief change, and where apparent agreement does not persist. Avoid interpreting personal motives from polite conduct alone.

No data collection, intervention, longitudinal monitoring, or experiment is authorized here. Relevant literature on joint attention, procedural justice, epistemic trust, motivated reasoning, and causal process tracing should be considered before claiming novelty.

## Relation to research program

This working note proposes an application of **question-dependent adequacy** and **independent relational complexity** to interactions among people. It must not promote a private anecdote into a general psychological law.

> **Preserving every component does not necessarily preserve the interactions that make those components consequential.**
